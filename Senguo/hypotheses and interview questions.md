# Hypotheses

---

## Hypothesis 1: Awareness of Services

**Hypothesis**  
We believe that students are unaware of the full range of well-being programs and support services available to them because the information is scattered across multiple platforms and not well advertised.

**Verification Method**  
To verify that, we will interview students and review existing service portals to see how many can name more than three services.  
And measure the percentage of students who can correctly identify available services (counselling, financial aid, food security programs, health clinics, etc.).

**Success Criteria**  
We are right if fewer than 50% of students can name at least three support services unaided.

**Interview Questions**  
- What well-being services do you know about that the university offers?  
- How did you find out about them?  
- Have you ever struggled to find information about support services?

---

## Hypothesis 2: Barriers to Access

**Hypothesis**  
We believe that students are less likely to access well-being services if they feel the process is complicated or requires visiting multiple websites/offices.

**Verification Method**  
To verify that, we will interview students who have used or considered using support services.  
And measure reported difficulties in accessing services (navigation time, multiple logins, long wait times).

**Success Criteria**  
We are right if more than 60% of students say they had difficulty knowing where to start or dropped out of the process due to complexity.

**Interview Questions**  
- Can you describe your experience when trying to access a well-being service?  
- Was the process easy or confusing? Why?  
- Have you ever given up seeking help because it was too hard to access?

---

## Hypothesis 3: After-hours Support Gap

**Hypothesis**  
We believe that students are especially underserved outside of business hours because many well-being services are only available 9–5 on weekdays.

**Verification Method**  
To verify that, we will ask students if they have faced challenges accessing help during evenings or weekends.  
And measure how many report needing support outside of normal hours and what alternatives they used.

**Success Criteria**  
We are right if at least 40% of students report needing well-being support outside business hours in the past 12 months.

**Interview Questions**  
- Have you ever needed well-being support outside of standard university hours?  
- What did you do in that situation?  
- Would you find it useful to have a tool that connects you to after-hours support automatically?

---

## Hypothesis 4: Food Security & Basic Needs

**Hypothesis**  
We believe that students experiencing food insecurity or financial stress are reluctant to ask for help because of stigma and lack of clear information on available aid.

**Verification Method**  
To verify that, we will survey students anonymously about food and financial stress.  
And measure the percentage of students who report food insecurity and whether they accessed support.

**Success Criteria**  
We are right if more than 30% of students report food insecurity but fewer than half of them used available programs.

**Interview Questions**  
- Have you ever worried about having enough food or money for essentials?  
- Did you know that food relief or financial aid programs exist?  
- What might stop you from using them?

---

## Hypothesis 5: Social Connection & Peer Support

**Hypothesis**  
We believe that students’ overall well-being is negatively affected by feelings of social isolation, and many are unaware of peer support networks or student-led initiatives.

**Verification Method**  
To verify that, we will:
- Interview students about their sense of belonging and awareness of peer groups.  
- Measure the percentage of students who report loneliness and whether they know about peer mentoring programs, student clubs, or community events.

**Success Criteria**  
We are right if:
- More than 40% of students report feeling isolated, **and**  
- Fewer than half have engaged with peer support opportunities.

**Interview Questions**  
- Can you tell me about a time when you felt connected—or disconnected—from the university community?  
- What kinds of peer groups, clubs, or networks (if any) have you come across? How did you find out about them?  
- If you wanted to meet other students or build supportive friendships, where would you start?  
- What would make it easier or more appealing for you to join peer support or community activities?  
- What would make it easier for you to connect with peers or join support groups?

## Interview Question Bank (General)

- What comes to mind when you hear “student well-being services”?  
- If you needed help with stress, food, or finances, where would you go first?  
- Can you tell me about a time you tried to access well-being support?  
- What made it easy or difficult?  
- Do you think the university communicates support options effectively?  
- Would a single app/portal that connects you to all services be useful? Why or why not?  
- What features would make you more likely to use such a tool?
